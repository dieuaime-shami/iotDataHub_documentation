# Getting Started

This guide walks you through the full IoTDataHub workflow — from creating an account to controlling your hardware online. No DevOps expertise required.

## 1. Create your account

Visit [iotdatahub.rw](https://www.iotdatahub.rw/) and sign up for a free account. No credit card is required for the free tier.

<figure className="iot-screenshot iot-screenshot--narrow">
  <img src="/screenshots/create_account.png" alt="IoTDataHub sign-up page" />
  <figcaption>Sign up with your email or Google account to get started.</figcaption>
</figure>

After signing in, you are taken to the **Dashboard**.

## 2. Create a channel

A **channel** defines how your hardware connects to IoTDataHub. You must create one before registering a device.

1. Open **Channels** in the sidebar
2. Click **+ Add New Channel**

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/creating_channel.png" alt="IoTDataHub Channels page with Add New Channel button" />
  <figcaption>The Channels page — click **+ Add New Channel** to open the creation form.</figcaption>
</figure>

3. Fill in the **Create New Channel** form:

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/Create_and_add_channel.png" alt="Create New Channel form" />
  <figcaption>Enter a channel name, select your hardware and connection type, and add a short description.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Channel Name** — a descriptive name (e.g. `blinking`, `temperature-sensors`)
- **Hardware** — select your microcontroller platform

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/select_the_hardware.png" alt="Hardware selection dropdown in Create New Channel form" />
  <figcaption>Choose your hardware: ESP32, ESP8266, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, or Other.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Connection Type** — how the device connects to the internet

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/Select_the_connectionType.png" alt="Connection type dropdown in Create New Channel form" />
  <figcaption>Select WiFi, Ethernet, Satellite, or GSM depending on your setup.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Description** — optional notes about the channel (up to 128 characters)

4. Click **Add Channel** to save

See the [Channels guide](/data-channels/channels) for naming tips and management details.

Once your channel exists, register a device linked to it.

1. Open **Devices** in the sidebar
2. Click **+ New Device**

3. Choose **From Channel**

  <img src="/screenshots/add_newDevice.png" alt="New Device dialog with From Channel option" />
  <figcaption>Select **From Channel** to link a device to an existing channel.</figcaption>

4. Select your channel and enter a **Device Name**

  <img src="/screenshots/select_the_channel.png" alt="From Channel form with channel selection" />

5. Choose a **Device Type** (Sensor, Actuator, Gateway, Controller, or Other)

6. Click **Create Device**

Your new device appears in the device list.

## 4. Build your online control panel

Each device has a **Widget Box** — a virtual control panel that mirrors your physical hardware. You build it by dragging widgets onto the canvas.

1. Click your device in the list to open its control page
2. The **Widget Box** panel on the right lists available widgets

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/widgetbox_section.png" alt="Widget Box panel with empty canvas" />
  <figcaption>Drag widgets from the Widget Box onto the canvas to build your control panel.</figcaption>
</figure>

3. Drag widgets (Switch, Slider, LED, etc.) onto the canvas

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/put_WidgetBox.png" alt="Widgets placed on the device canvas" />
  <figcaption>Place widgets on the canvas — each one maps to a virtual pin on your microcontroller.</figcaption>
</figure>

4. Click each widget to configure it:
   - **Title** — the label shown on the dashboard
   - **Datastream** — the virtual pin your hardware reads or writes
   - **On/Off values** — the values sent when toggling (e.g. `1` and `0`)
   - **Theme** — visual style for the widget

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/add_wiget_data.png" alt="Switch widget configuration dialog" />
  <figcaption>Configure each widget's name, virtual pin, values, and theme, then click **Save Configuration**.</figcaption>
</figure>

5. Click **Save & Apply** to save your control panel

## 5. Install the library and upload your sketch

### Install the Arduino library

1. Open Arduino IDE
2. Go to **Sketch → Include Library → Manage Libraries**
3. Search for **IoTDataHub**
4. Click **Install**

Supported hardware: **Arduino**, **ESP32**, **ESP8266**, **Raspberry Pi**, and any HTTP-capable device. See [Supported Hardware](/connect-devices/supported-hardware).

### Copy your device configuration

On your device's control page, click **Config** to open the **Device Configuration** dialog. Copy the credentials shown.

<figure className="iot-screenshot">
  <img src="/screenshots/device_configuration.png" alt="Device Configuration dialog with credentials" />
  <figcaption>Copy your username, organization, device token, and device ID — use **Copy All** to grab everything at once.</figcaption>
</figure>

Paste these values into your Arduino sketch along with your WiFi credentials. The library reads the `#define` values automatically:

```cpp
#include <IoTDataHub.h>

// Paste from Device Configuration (Config button)
#define IOTDATAHUB_USER_NAME         "Your Name"
#define IOTDATAHUB_ORGANIZATION_NAME "My Work Space"
#define IOTDATAHUB_DEVICE_TOKEN      "your-device-token"
#define IOTDATAHUB_DEVICE_ID         "your-device-id"

// Your WiFi network
const char* ssid     = "YOUR_WIFI_NAME";
const char* password = "YOUR_WIFI_PASSWORD";

IoTDataHub iot;

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  WiFi.begin(ssid, password);
  while (WiFi.status() != WL_CONNECTED) { delay(500); }
  iot.begin();
}

void loop() {
  iot.run(); // Keeps the device connected and syncs widget states
}
```

Make sure your **WiFi name and password are correct**, then upload the sketch to your microcontroller.

Full library reference: [Arduino Library](/connect-devices/arduino-library).

## 6. Go online and control your device

After uploading, your device connects to IoTDataHub. Return to the device page in your browser — the status indicator turns **online**, and your widgets become interactive.

Toggle switches, adjust sliders, and read sensor values directly from the dashboard. Every action on the web control panel is sent to your physical hardware in real time.
